      The read

      The room grew, then settled. June's 10.2 average jumped to 15.2 in July and held at 15.6 in August. September came in at 13.3 across 3 classes (Sep 6 skipped for Labor Day).

      Judge pay on the running average. Unlimited-pass money settles about a month late, so single months swing: August's $101.40 carried July's catch-up, September's $77.56 is likely missing its share. Averaged, pay has held at $92–96 per class since July.

      The growth lever is the second visit. Of 64 people since June, 36 came once and only 3 came exactly twice. Whoever comes back usually stays. August brought 18 newcomers and only 2 returned.

      September dipped on lost regulars, not fewer newcomers. Twelve people with 3+ classes weren't seen in September, including drop-in regulars, the best-paying visit type.

      The gap, explained

      July's fuller room with flat pay looked alarming. Michelle confirmed why: pay is per-visit value, and the dip was mix. August recovered.

      Confirmed: per-visit value, not cash-basis. Michelle confirmed (Sep 2026) the 50/50 is calculated per attendance — each check-in is worth half of that person's per-class price. A 5-class pass bought for $80 is $16/class, so Charles gets $8 for that visit. It was never a monthly cash-basis split.

      The July dip was mix, not timing. July drew more $0 attendees (free intros) and more unlimited / heavy-pass users, whose per-class value is very low. Same rough pass/drop-in split, but a lower average value per head. August's mix normalized and rev/check-in recovered to $13.00.

      Takeaway: headcount is not payout. Drop-ins and fresh 5-class passes pay the most per head; free intros pay nothing; heavy unlimited users pay little. Track attendance and $/class as two separate lines.
