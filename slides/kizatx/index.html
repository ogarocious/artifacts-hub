      The read

      The room got dramatically bigger. June's average class size of 10.2 grew to 15.2 in July, a +49% jump. Peak class went from 13 to 19.

      Charles-taught classes grew even faster. June 7 was a sub (Alina). Excluding it, Charles's own head count went from 32 across 3 classes to 61 across 4 classes, a +91% jump month over month.

      The payout didn't follow. June $362.75 to July $377.43 is +4%. Revenue per check-in fell from $17.70 to $12.37, a −30% drop. Charles's gut ("July should have been more") was right.

      The gap has a name. Cash-basis 50/50 split. If July classes are full of people burning passes bought in earlier months, the revenue credited to July stays thin even as the room fills. Question is out to Michelle.

      Attendance grew, payout didn't

      The room roughly doubled in density. The payout barely moved. Revenue per check-in dropped 30%.

      The gap, month over month
      Month Check-ins Payout Implied gross (×2) Rev / check-in
      June 2026 41 $362.75 $725.50 $17.70
      July 2026 61 $377.43 $754.86 $12.37

      Best-fit hypothesis: cash-basis 50/50 split. When a student burns a pass they bought in April on a class in July, Esquina's July revenue doesn't include that visit. The cash was already booked to April. If July is dense with pass-burners from earlier months, July's "revenue" stays flat even as the room fills.

      Composition supports the hypothesis. Passes were about 55% of both months' check-ins. So June to July isn't a mix shift; it's the same mix at higher volume. The 20 extra check-ins in July had to be paying at a lower average rate — consistent with pass redemptions that already cleared cash in earlier months.

      Question is out to Michelle. Alternate explanations to rule out: unusual comp density, instructor-rate change, or new-student $70 unlimited eating margin.
