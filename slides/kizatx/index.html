      The read

      June was the inflection. Per-class earnings jumped from $68.81 to $90.69, a real +31.8% step and not a calendar artifact.

      July held it. Nudged up to $94.36 per class. The residency didn't regress after the June jump.

      The raw-dollar story is misleading. Total payouts are +66% since April, but per-class is +24.5%. Most of the "growth" between April and May was just teaching one extra Sunday.

      The unknown is the room. Reverse-math on July implies 9–17 paid heads per Sunday. Gut says the room felt bigger than that. Comp policy visibility is the missing signal.

      Why the two charts diverge

      April had 3 Sundays taught (skipped Easter). May–July had 4 each. Raw dollars conflate calendar with performance; $/class isolates performance. Apr→May: raw looks like +21% growth, but per-class is actually a −9% dip.

      What we don't know

      Data gaps that would sharpen this tracker. Ordered by expected upside.

      • Ask
        Per-Sunday headcount from Michelle. She has it (the 7/5 correction proves it). If she'll include a per-Friday-and-Sunday headcount + paid-vs-comp split in future breakdowns, this deck becomes a real attendance tracker instead of a payout ledger.
      • Ask
        Package vs. drop-in mix. The reverse-math table on slide 6 collapses to a single number the moment we know how many students are on drop-in vs. 5-pack vs. 10-pack vs. unlimited. Even a rough monthly breakdown would work.
      • Ask
        Comp policy visibility. Who counts as comped: instructors, board, work-trade, promo guests? Without this, "felt headcount vs. paid-for" is always a hand-wave.
      • Watch
        Is the July $49 correction a one-off? Or does per-night attendance reconciliation happen regularly? Track how many corrections show up over the next 3 months.
      • Watch
        New-student $70 unlimited uptake. This is the biggest gross-per-head suppressor on the pricing sheet. If lots of newcomers land on it, per-class $ can stagnate even while headcount grows.
