      Three ways this ends.

      Not exhaustive. But the real branches of the decision tree.

      Path A · Independent

      Keep building. Watch them.

      • Zero risk to your capital or focus
      • You keep 100% of upside
      • Their thin editorial + missing E-E-A-T stays their problem
      • Depth strategy compounds; scale strategy doesn't
      • You leave their 31k SEO URLs on the table
      • APAC market stays out of reach
      • i18n / apps built from scratch if you go global
      • Doesn't answer Matt (may sour him)
      Path B · Partner

      Non-overlapping trade.

      • You license artist-portal / glossary / pedagogy tech to them
      • They cross-list your artists into APAC catalog
      • Referral fee both directions
      • Learn their numbers without buying anything
      • Low capital, reversible
      • Complicated ownership + branding
      • If he really wants out, partnership won't scratch it
      • Distraction cost is real (integration, coordination)
      • May not survive his exit
      Path C · Acquire

      Buy the platform.

      • Instant 31k-URL SEO footprint absorbed
      • APAC + i18n infrastructure baked in
      • Two native apps (iOS + Android) already published
      • Public API + OpenAPI + llms.txt strategy inherited
      • Removes a competitor
      • Only rational if revenue justifies the price
      • Integration cost = months of engineering
      • Two codebases, two brand identities to reconcile
      • May inherit tech debt you can't see from outside
      • If revenue is $0-50k, "acquisition" is really an asset-buy
      • Focus tax: pulls you off depth strategy

      Questions to ask before anything else.

      In this order. Refuse to negotiate anything until you get real answers to at least the first six.

      MUST-ANSWER · GATES EVERYTHING BELOW

      • ◆ What is your trailing 12-month gross revenue and net revenue? If he won't share, the conversation ends here. Everything else is guesswork.
      • ◆ What is your monthly ticket volume, both count and gross dollars? 7% take rate is meaningless without volume behind it.
      • ◆ Trailing MRR trend: up, flat, or declining over the last 6 months? Direction matters more than absolute number.
      • ◆ What are your monthly hosting + third-party costs? Next.js + CloudFront + Postgres + email + apps + API costs add up. Net margin is what matters.
      • ◆ Why sell now, specifically? What's the "something else"? Answer reveals leverage. If he's burned out, price softens. If he's chasing a new opportunity, price hardens.
      • ◆ Who owns the code, the brand, the apps, and the data? Any co-founder equity issues? Delaware LLC with two founders. You need cap table clarity before any transaction talk.

      FOLLOW-UPS · IF THE FIRST SIX GO WELL

      • ◇ What's the split between organizer accounts you'd characterize as "active" vs. "signed up once"? Vanity vs. real engagement.
      • ◇ If we walk away today, what's your Plan B? Sell to whom else, keep running, wind down? Frames urgency. Also reveals whether he's talking to other buyers.
      • ◇ Would you be open to a data-room review under NDA before we discuss structure? Filters serious sellers from tire-kickers.
      • ◇ If "partner" is more interesting than "sell", what does a good partnership actually look like to you? Let him define it. Notice if he can.

      The read.

      Default to Path B (partnership), but only after the numbers slide is filled in.

      Their tech is genuinely good. Their brand is thin. Their overlap with you is ~15%. That combination is a licensing or integration opportunity, not an acquisition target, unless the P&L makes it embarrassingly cheap. If it does, revisit Path C with cold eyes about integration cost.

      You said you're not sure where the overlap is. The audit says: barely. You're not competing with him, you're both solving adjacent parts of the same problem. Which means the interesting question isn't "should I buy this?" It's "what can we trade so we both win?"
