# The Room Doubled in Four Months

*Notes on a Sunday Kizomba practica in East Austin, and the work behind the number.*

I run a Sunday Kizomba practica in East Austin, at Esquina Tango. My teaching partner Alina and I lead it together every week. In April, 26 people showed up across the month. In July, 61 did. That is a room that more than doubled, and I want to be honest about how it happened, because it was not an accident.

## The numbers

April was the quiet baseline: 26 monthly check-ins. May crept up to 31. June measured 41. July finished at 61, with a single Sunday peak of 19 on July 19. The line does not look dramatic on any one week. It compounds. That is the interesting shape.

## What actually worked

I did not run a single paid ad. There was no boosted post, no sponsored campaign, no marketing budget in play. What I did was three specific things, week after week, and I want to name them because the word "growth" gets thrown around too easily.

### 1. A monthly calendar, up front.

Every month I publish a graphic that lays out every Sunday of the month with its topic. It goes to the Kiz ATX site, which lives on Where Can We Dance, the platform I built, and gets reposted across our owned channels. The point is not the graphic itself. The point is that people can plan four Sundays ahead instead of finding out about the next practica on Saturday night. A dance community is a scheduling problem before it is anything else. When the calendar is visible, more people show up.

### 2. A WhatsApp room where members vote on the topic.

We have a WhatsApp community for regulars and newcomers. Once a month I put up the candidate topics and let the room vote. That does two things at once. It gives me real signal about what people want to work on, which is more useful than what I think they should work on. And it turns members into participants in the calendar, not just recipients of it. When you help pick the topic, you show up for it. That is not a trick. That is human.

### 3. Two instructors, sustained feedback, beginners genuinely welcomed.

Alina teaches every Sunday alongside me. She is a teaching partner, not a substitute. What that means practically is that leads get real feedback across an entire practica, which is uncommon at drop-in socials, and follows get a second set of eyes on connection and axis. It also means the floor sees two people modeling actual dancing, not just talking through steps. We keep the door wide open for beginners because Kizomba is meant to be shared, and because there is a difference between "all levels welcome" as a marketing phrase and "beginners are actually welcomed" as a room culture.

## What this is, and what it is not

Kizomba is Angolan, and Kizomba is deeply diasporic. Both are true, and I mean to hold both.

Geographically the dance grew out of Semba, from Angola. That root matters, and I will name it every time. Musically the story is bigger. Kizomba was shaped by Zouk out of the French Antilles, Guadeloupe and Martinique, where Kassav made the sound travel. It was reshaped by Cabo Love and ghetto zouk out of Cape Verde. It carries four hundred years of Portuguese colonial presence in Angola in its bones, whether we name that or not. And in its American life, it lives alongside R&B and Hip-Hop, because those are the sounds Black dancers here are listening to and moving to on any given Sunday.

Urban Kiz, which is what we practice on Sundays, is the current evolution of all of that. It was named and codified in France, but its roots are not European. Its roots are Black, Antillean, Cape Verdean, Angolan, and Black American. It is not a fun Latin dance, and I will not describe it that way.

Kiz ATX is Black-owned, and part of what I do every week is honor the full lineage: the Angolan origin, the Antillean and Cape Verdean musical inheritance, the Black artists who have carried this work across continents, and the ongoing conversation between those roots and Black American music that keeps the dance alive here. That framing is not a footnote to the growth. It is the reason people who are looking for this specific room can find it and stay.

The 61 in July is not a finish line. It is a room that trusts the calendar, trusts the teaching, and has become a place people plan around. Doubling is nice, but the more interesting metric is that the July peak of 19 in a single class was not a ceiling. It was a Sunday. The next Sunday will happen.
