Rubik + a body face.

Four sans candidates that pair well with Rubik and have real italics (not synthetic browser-slant). Each shows a Rubik 800 heading for reference, a body paragraph, and an italic kick sample. Italic is where a body font tells you if it can carry your voice.

IBM Plex Sans
Google Fonts · Sans · Distinctive

Workhorse body sans with real character. Distinctive cuts on the c, g, l, and a. Slightly technical, slightly architectural. Was designed to carry IBM's whole editorial system, so it's built to do long reading without going invisible. Italic has real personality.

Work Sans
Google Fonts · Sans · Humanist

Humanist grotesque designed for on-screen reading. Warm, approachable, essayistic. Reads like a person is talking to you, not a machine. Pairs cleanly with Rubik's rounded warmth without competing. Italic is quiet and clear.

DM Sans
Google Fonts · Sans · Restrained

Modern geometric with the personality dial turned down. Cleanest of the four. Reads as understated and contemporary. Best pick if you want the type to disappear and let the writing lead. Slightly more austere than Rubik, which provides real hierarchy contrast.

Barlow
Google Fonts · Sans · Slightly condensed

Slightly condensed grotesque. More space-efficient, letters pack tighter without feeling cramped. Different PROPORTION from Rubik, which is where the pairing contrast comes from. Feels editorial and functional at once. Nice italic slant.

My pick
Work Sans for your voice. It reads warm and human, which matches how you actually write. Italic is clean without being decorative, so kick lines and deks land. It sits comfortably alongside Rubik without competing (same humanist family, different job). IBM Plex Sans is the strong #2 if you want more visible character in the body: Plex has real personality (some love it, some feel it's too technical). Skip DM Sans if you want the type to have any voice; skip Barlow unless you specifically want condensed proportions.
