      Everything else is built on this

      The run has three blocks.

      A · Opening weekend
      2 nights
      • Sat Oct 31 · Halloween, the brand moment
      • Sun Nov 1 · evening block

      Clocks fall back at 2:00am on Nov 1, so the Halloween social runs five real hours, not four.

      B · Midweek
      4 nights
      • Mon Nov 2 to Wed Nov 4 · socials from 7:00pm
      • Thu Nov 5 · keynote, no dancing

      The lowest-value block per night, and the one that changes character most with the venue.

      C · Closing weekend
      3 nights
      • Fri Nov 6 · weekend party
      • Sat Nov 7 · daytime plus night
      • Sun Nov 8 · closing

      The marquee. Two of these three nights need a 2:00am close and neither Saturday has a venue yet.

      Six contiguous spans are possible across three blocks: A, B, C, A+B, B+C and A+B+C. We are building five of them. Which one we drop, and why, is the next slide.

      Passes include classes

      Every pass carries the workshops in its span. Single nights never do. That one rule means we never have to price a day by what happens inside it, which matters while the venue is unknown.

      Per night falls as you buy more

      $50 for a weekend night down to $41 across the full week. The midweek block sits low at $28 because those evenings are practicas as much as parties.

      The ceiling is Charles's call

      $350 is the hard ceiling on the full week and it scales down if the programme shrinks. Six programmed nights means $275. Four means $200 and a rename.

      Six spans exist, five get built

      We are not selling A+B.

      Opening weekend into the week

      Oct 31 through Nov 5, going home before the closing weekend. That buyer flies in for Halloween, does the midweek block, then leaves on the Friday the marquee nights start.

      Almost nobody travels for the quiet half and skips the loud one. Every product we add is another price we have to defend against every other combination, so this one costs more in complexity than it returns.

      B+C earns its place

      Halloween falls on a Saturday. A real share of the North American market has commitments at home that night and cannot land until the Monday. The Summit Pass at $250 is built for exactly that person.

      If someone genuinely wants A+B they buy the Opening Weekend and Midweek passes separately at $195 total. Nobody is turned away, we simply do not print a card for it.

      There is a platform reason too. Where Can We Dance has no bundle or add-on concept, so every span has to be its own ticket row on the page. Five pass cards plus three single-night types is already eight things to read before deciding. Nine is worse, and the ninth would sell to almost nobody.

      Every bundle wins

      Full week beats its parts by $20. Summit beats its parts by $10. Weekend Pass beats three separate nights by $30 and adds the classes on top.

      The weeknight moved to $25

      At $20 the all-singles path lands at $320, which is five dollars under the full week, and only the classes rule holds the line. $25 restores a real $20 cushion. It also returns to the number in the superseded ladder.

      What to watch

      Every discount on a single night narrows these gaps. Do not discount single nights. Discount the passes if anything, because that is the direction that keeps the ladder coherent.

      A different product for a different audience

      Single nights are social only.

      Two audiences, two products

      Roughly 70% of the waitlist is travelling in, 30 flying and 10 driving, against only nine Austin locals. Someone who has already sunk $400 to $800 in flights and lodging is not buying a single Tuesday. The passes are for them.

      Single nights are for the Kiz ATX group, 787 members deep, who decide on the day. That is community access and walk-up revenue, not the core business.

      Run the door, and hold seats back for it

      The door is roughly 20 to 35% above advance, which teaches the market that waiting costs money and pulls sales forward. Door cash is also the live contingency fund.

      Hold back roughly 15 spots per marquee night so a walk-up crowd cannot overfill the room. On the platform this means capping the public tier low and taking walk-ups through the organiser's manual order path.

      Why $199 and not $275

      $199 reads as a launch number rather than a discount. The early bird already exists at $275, so the founding tier has to be a different kind of thing, not a slightly better version of the same thing.

      What it costs

      Roughly $760 against selling those ten at the early bird price. That is the whole exposure, and it buys a full room at launch and ten people who tell the story for us.

      Status, not just price

      Named on the printed programme and on signage, and first refusal on 2027 at Year 1 pricing. The recognition costs nothing and it is what makes the tier repeatable next year.

      The honest caveat

      The waitlist survey never captured price tolerance. $199 is a considered guess, not a measured number. A one-question follow-up to the 57 signups would settle it, takes an hour to build, and needs no venue. It is the highest-value action available right now. If it comes back showing comfort at $400 and above, this whole ladder should be revisited before anything locks.

      Everything is relative to the venue signature

      How sales open.

      1. 0
        Venue signs. The gate. No ticket sales and no major announcements before this, and both Saturdays are still open. Every asset below is built and staged in advance so the on-sale is a switch, not a project.
      2. 1
        Day 0 · the 39 go first. The Founding Ten tier goes live hidden, unlocked by an access code on a link. That link goes only to the committed waitlist names. They get 48 hours alone with the ten seats.
      3. 2
        Day 2 · public launch. The same tier is unhidden. Whatever founding seats are left go to the multi-channel blast. The page shows a live count of seats remaining.
      4. 3
        Day 7 or sell-out · Early Bird takes over. The card advances to $275 on its own. Nothing has to be switched by hand, and no buyer ever sees a dead tier.
      5. 4
        Oct 1 · Standard at $325. Roughly four weeks of run-up at the full price, which is where most of the volume should land.
      6. 5
        Oct 30 · advance closes, door opens. Door pricing runs 20 to 35% above advance, on the seats deliberately held back.
      Refund guarantee on the founding seats

      Full refund if the venue or the programmed nights change materially. It costs nothing if we deliver, and it is the honest answer to asking someone to buy before the room is certain. The refund policy is captured on every order at checkout.

      Three platform fixes come first

      Abandoned carts currently hold seats forever, the seats-remaining badge shows capacity rather than what is left, and the capacity check has a gap two simultaneous buyers can slip through. On a ten-seat tier the first two will bite. All three are small and all three ship before on-sale.

      Outside the paid ladder entirely

      The cohort does not buy tickets.

      How they get in

      A hidden tier at zero cost, capped at 25, unlocked by a single access code. Laurent holds the code and passes it to the cohort. They never see the public pricing page and nothing they do touches the paid inventory.

      • Festival access is part of the Evokeez package
      • Housing and some group meals covered
      • Flights on Evokeez
      Why it is modelled this way

      The cohort is cost, not revenue. Keeping them on a separate free tier means the revenue model stays honest and every number on the next slide describes only the 100 paying attendees.

      It also gives us a clean headcount for the room. 100 paying plus roughly 25 cohort is ~125 in the room, which is the figure every venue conversation is built on.

      Cohort package pricing is coordinated through Laurent and is not public. Nothing about it appears on the ticketing page, in the ladder, or in any marketing. The hidden tier is purely the mechanism for getting 25 people through the door with a check-in code each.

      The block passes pay for themselves

      Adding the Summit, Midweek and Opening Weekend passes lifts the planning number roughly $1,000, because they convert people who would otherwise have bought two or three single nights, or nothing at all.

      The grant carries the event either way

      Price is a positioning decision, not cost recovery. If the venue lands at $11,000 instead of $7,000 that moves the housing cap, not the ticket price. Against an oversubscribed waitlist, underpricing and filling the room beats overpricing and running at 60.
