      What your site promises

      I read the whole thing before writing any of this. Three promises do the heavy lifting.

      SPEED

      Live in days, not months.

      Three steps: you learn the firm, you configure and test, they go live.

      FIT

      No rip and replace.

      Kova drops into the stack the firm already runs. Nothing gets migrated.

      REACH

      Twenty plus systems.

      Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther, Filevine, Captorra, Lawmatics, HighLevel, Salesforce, HubSpot and more on the wall.

      Those are the right promises for this market. Together they also describe the single most expensive product to deliver: bespoke fit, in days, across a long list of destinations.

      You already said the part most people miss: booking the appointment and handing the firm a spreadsheet to re-key would look amateur at your price point. Agreed. Here is what sits underneath that.

      The number is firm ten, not firm one

      At your numbers, per firm, per month, the revenue side is fine. The thing that decides whether Kova is a company or a very good freelance gig is what the tenth onboarding costs you. Not in dollars first. In your evenings.

      BESPOKE PER FIRM

      Each new firm is a fresh integration project. New fields, new routing rules, new edge cases, a new round trip with the offshore team.

      • Firm 1  full build
      • Firm 5  full build
      • Firm 10 full build

      Your cost per client stays flat. Your margin is capped by your calendar, and growth makes it worse instead of better.

      SHARED SUBSTRATE

      One intake model, built once. Each new firm is practice areas, calendars, routing rules and field mappings filled in against it.

      • Firm 1  full build
      • Firm 5  configuration
      • Firm 10 configuration

      Cost per client falls with every firm you add. "Live in days" stops being a marketing line and becomes a fact about how the thing is built.

      Same wall of twenty logos. In the first case it is a liability, because every logo is a promise someone has to build under pressure. In the second it is the moat.

      The cost is not the API call

      Pushing a record into Clio or GHL is a couple of hours of work. What eats the week is deciding what the record should say before you send it, and what happens when the caller does not cooperate. You put it well: the logic has to be thought through first.

      ◆

      New lead or existing client

      Two totally different records, two different destinations, and the caller will not announce which one they are.

      ◆

      Which matter type

      "My mom passed and there is a house" is estate. Sometimes it is probate litigation. The firm cares about the difference.

      ◆

      Whose calendar

      Matter type maps to an attorney, attorney maps to a calendar, and the mapping is different at every firm.

      ◆

      Conflict check

      If the opposing party is already a client, that consult should not get booked. This one has real consequences.

      ◆

      Fuzzy identity

      A caller says "Rob Miller" and the CRM holds Robert Miller, Roberta Miller and R. Miller. Match, create, or ask.

      ◆

      Who catches the fallback

      When the agent cannot resolve it, something has to hold the call rather than drop it. That path needs an owner.

      Every one of these is the same question at every firm. The answers differ. That split is exactly what makes it buildable once and configurable after.

      One call, all the way through

      Using the estate planning inquiry from your own homepage. Traced into GHL, because GHL is the one I run in production and can be held to.

      ONE CANONICAL RECORD

      {
  caller:      { name, phone, email }
  intent:      new_client | existing | other
  matter_type: estate_planning
  confidence:  0.0 to 1.0
  urgency:     routine | deadline | emergency
  assigned_to: resolved from matter_type
  conflict:    clear | flagged | unchecked
  booking:     { slot, calendar_id, confirmed }
  transcript:  url
  fallback:    null | reason
}

      The agent's only job is to fill this in honestly, including the confidence score and the parts it could not resolve. Everything downstream reads from here.

      WHERE IT LANDS IN GHL

      callerContact, created or matched on phone first, not name
      matter_typeCustom field plus a tag, so pipelines and automations can both read it
      intentOpportunity in the intake pipeline, stage set by intent
      bookingAppointment on the resolved calendar, not a generic one
      transcriptNote on the contact, so staff see it without leaving the record
      fallbackTask assigned to a human, with the reason attached

      Clio and Lawmatics take the same record. Contact becomes a Clio contact and a matter, or a Lawmatics contact and a pipeline entry. The record does not change. Only the adapter does. That is the whole idea, and it is why the tenth firm is cheaper than the first.

      The four branches that decide trust

      A demo call goes clean. Real calls do not. What a firm is actually buying is what happens on the messy ones, and this is where an intake layer either earns the retainer or quietly loses it.

      Partial name match

      Do not guess. Write the contact as new, tag it possible_duplicate, link the candidates, and let a human merge. A wrong merge in a legal CRM is much worse than a duplicate.

      Low confidence on matter type

      Book the consult anyway, route to the firm's default intake attorney, and flag it. Never lose the appointment over a classification you can fix in ten seconds.

      Possible conflict

      Capture everything, book nothing, notify immediately. This is the one branch where the safe default is to stop, and the firm will want to hear you say that out loud.

      The write fails

      Queue and retry, and if it still fails, the record surfaces somewhere a person will actually look. A call that got answered but never landed is worse than a missed call, because nobody knows it happened.

      Four rules, written once, inherited by every firm after. The firm-specific part is only who gets notified and what the defaults are.

Retell and the prompting are yours, you have already shipped that, and you are better at it than I would be for a while.       On the sales calls too

      You said it is good to have a technical person on the call telling the firm what is and is not possible. I agree, and I would go further. It matters most that the person saying yes on the call is the same person who has to build it on Monday.

      That wall of twenty logos is set up for exactly one failure: a firm asks about Filevine, someone says yes because the logo is on the site, and now it exists as a promise before it exists as software. If I am on the call, I do not let that happen, because I am the one who eats it.

      Same person on the call and on the build is not two jobs. It is the thing that keeps the "live in days" claim honest as you add firms.
